# Project Vision

Orchestral lowers the boundary of being a super experimentalist from every possible aspect using AI agents.

## The Problem

LabVIEW has been the default tool for experimental physics labs for decades. From a physicist's perspective, its limitations are not just software inconveniences -- they are barriers that prevent researchers from doing their best work.

**Hardware knowledge barrier.** Every new device integration requires deep protocol-level knowledge -- serial commands, SDK calls, timing quirks. A physicist who wants to add a new sensor to their experiment must either become a part-time embedded systems engineer or wait weeks for someone who already knows the hardware. This knowledge lives in people's heads and leaves when they do.

**No top-down design guidance.** LabVIEW lets you wire things together, but it never asks whether your experiment design makes sense. There is no stage where you describe what you want to measure, what conditions matter, and what devices you need before you start building. You jump straight into implementation details without a plan.

**No data or version management.** Raw data files accumulate in folders with ad-hoc naming conventions. There is no structured link between a dataset and the experiment configuration that produced it. When something changes -- a parameter, a device, a procedure -- there is no version history to trace what was different.

**No automatic metadata.** Which calibration was active? What was the ambient temperature? Who started the run? These facts matter for reproducibility, but LabVIEW does not capture them unless someone manually adds logging code for each one.

**No decision logging.** Why did you change the Reynolds number range last Tuesday? Why did you switch from Camera A to Camera B? These decisions happen in conversations, emails, and notebooks that are never linked to the experimental record.

**No automatic reporting.** After a day of runs, someone has to manually compile what happened, what worked, and what needs attention. This reporting tax discourages frequent review and lets problems go unnoticed.

**No lab knowledge sharing.** When a postdoc leaves, their hard-won knowledge about equipment quirks, calibration procedures, and experimental tricks leaves with them. LabVIEW programs encode some of this implicitly, but it is locked inside graphical code that is difficult to read, search, or transfer.

**Graphical code is hostile to AI.** LabVIEW's dataflow diagrams cannot be meaningfully read, analyzed, or generated by language models. This means the entire ecosystem of AI-assisted development, review, and automation is inaccessible. As AI becomes central to how software is built and maintained, LabVIEW code is a dead end.

## What Orchestral Is

Orchestral is two things that work as one.

**A platform that runs experiments.** It coordinates multiple hardware devices, manages acquisition timing, enforces safety conditions, and captures structured data with full metadata. This is the runtime -- the part that replaces what LabVIEW does during an experiment.

**An agent that helps you build, operate, and learn.** It guides experiment design from the top down, walks you through device integration step by step, logs decisions automatically, drafts reports, and preserves institutional knowledge. This is the AI layer -- the part that replaces what a perfect lab manager, embedded systems expert, and documentation discipline would do if you had unlimited time.

These two halves are not separate products. The platform generates the structured data and artifacts that the agent reasons about. The agent produces the configurations and procedures that the platform executes. They are designed to reinforce each other.

## Core Principles

1. **Top-down experiment design before device details.** You start by describing what you want to measure and under what conditions. Device selection and wiring come after the experiment structure is defined. The system guides this process so that design decisions are explicit, not implicit in implementation choices.

2. **AI-guided device integration.** Adding a new device to the platform should not require deep protocol expertise. The system provides step-by-step guidance through interface definition, service implementation, driver integration, and panel construction. This replaces the dependency on a dedicated e-machine-shop or the one person in the lab who knows the hardware.

3. **Structured data and version management from the initial skeleton.** From the moment a project is created, data output paths, naming conventions, and configuration versioning are in place. You do not add structure later -- it is there from the start.

4. **Automatic metadata logging linked to raw data.** Every run captures device states, calibration references, environmental conditions, operator identity, and timing information. This metadata is linked directly to the raw data it describes, not stored in a separate notebook.

5. **Decision logging via the daily dream process.** At the end of each working session, the system compiles a record of what changed and why, drawn from the day's activity. This requires zero additional effort from the researcher. The default is that decisions are logged, not forgotten.

6. **Auto-drafted experiment reports with a human review gate.** Daily and weekly summaries are generated automatically from run data, metadata, and decision logs. A human reviews and approves before anything is finalized. The goal is to eliminate the reporting tax while keeping the researcher in control.

7. **Lab-wide knowledge hub.** Equipment quirks, calibration procedures, experimental techniques, and troubleshooting steps are captured in a searchable, structured format that survives personnel changes. Knowledge belongs to the lab, not to individuals.

8. **Reproducibility as a first-class guarantee.** Every experiment run can be traced back to its exact configuration, calibration state, and software version. Reproducing a past result means loading a snapshot, not reconstructing conditions from memory.

9. **Calibration tracking per device per run.** Each device maintains a calibration history. Every run records which calibration was active for each device. Drift and recalibration events are part of the permanent record.

10. **Compliance and audit trail.** All configuration changes, run events, and operator actions are logged in an append-only record. This supports both internal quality control and external audit requirements.

11. **Text-first, AI-friendly architecture.** Configurations are YAML, not binary blobs. Protocols are structured text, not graphical diagrams. This makes every artifact readable, diffable, and accessible to language models for analysis, generation, and review.

12. **Extensible by design -- grow by addition, not rewrite.** New devices, new experiment types, and new analysis pipelines are added through well-defined extension points. The core architecture does not need to be modified to accommodate growth.

## Orchestral's Roles

Orchestral fills several roles that are currently either missing or filled by scarce human expertise.

**E-machine shop.** It guides device integration from specification to working panel, replacing the dependency on a dedicated hardware integration team or a single expert who knows every protocol.

**Experienced embedded systems engineer.** It understands communication protocols, timing constraints, and driver patterns well enough to walk a physicist through connecting new hardware without requiring them to become a protocol specialist.

**Experiment manager.** It enforces structure on experiment design, tracks progress across runs, maintains the link between goals and results, and surfaces problems before they compound.

**Lab-wide knowledge hub.** It captures, organizes, and serves institutional knowledge about equipment, procedures, and experimental techniques so that the lab's collective expertise is always accessible.

**Data-analysis copilot.** It works with the structured data and metadata that the platform produces to help researchers explore results, identify patterns, and prepare findings for publication.

## What Orchestral Is NOT

Orchestral does not provide capabilities that a super experimentalist could not achieve with LabVIEW and perfect discipline. A researcher with unlimited time, deep hardware knowledge, meticulous documentation habits, perfect institutional memory, and rigorous version control practices could, in principle, accomplish everything Orchestral offers.

The point is that this level of discipline is unrealistic for any individual and impossible to maintain across a team over time. Orchestral lowers the barrier to reach that level for every lab member. It makes the best practices automatic rather than aspirational, so that the quality of experimental work depends on the platform, not on the heroic effort of individuals.
